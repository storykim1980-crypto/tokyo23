Local Knowledge

Obvious to locals, invisible to visitors

There is a simple reason guidebooks leave this out: to the people who live here it is too obvious to be worth explaining.

串
How to order
The stew comes by the portion, not the piece

Sit down and they will ask whether one portion of nikomi is right for you. A portion is a set of skewers, mixing three cuts — gristle, intestine and lung. This is not a place where you pick cuts one by one. On a first visit, just say yes.

写
Unwritten rule
No photographs inside

Photography inside is expressly forbidden — not the crescent hinoki counter, not the stew pot. Take your picture of the frontage outside and then go in. Raising a camera without knowing the rule is the mistake foreign visitors make most often here.

時
Heads-up
Sources disagree on the hours and closing days

Everyone agrees it opens at four; closing is variously given as seven, eight or nine, and the rest days as Sunday-Monday-holidays or Monday-Thursday-Sunday-holidays. Old listings keep circulating. Since they shut when the pot is empty, the only sure approach is to arrive early.

五
The backstory
The &#x27;three great nikomi&#x27; came out of one book

It begins with Ota Kazuhiko&#x27;s 1990 book, which named Ohashi, Yamariki and Kishidaya as the three. Uchida in Tateishi and this shop were added later to make five. No body certified any of it; one writer&#x27;s list simply stuck.

What it is

A working-man&#x27;s tavern counted among Tokyo&#x27;s five great nikomi houses. It began in 1924 as a stall nearby, closed during the war, and reopened on this spot in 1947. The crescent-shaped hinoki counter was designed by the second-generation owner.

What to order

Skewered beef nikomi
The only skewered nikomi among Tokyo&#x27;s five great stews. Three cuts come on the sticks: gristle, intestine and lung.
About ¥130 a skewer
Egg soup
The stewing liquor with an egg dropped in. Most people order it last.
About ¥330
Umewari
Shochu with a splash of plum syrup, the old east-end way. You can ask for ice and a chaser.

Why locals keep coming

The &#x27;three great nikomi of Tokyo&#x27; are those named by Ota Kazuhiko in his 1990 book — Ohashi in Kita-senju, Yamariki in Morishita, Kishidaya in Tsukishima. Adding Uchida in Tateishi and this shop makes five. Osakaya is the only one to serve its stew on skewers.
