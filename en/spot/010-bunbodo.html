Local Knowledge

Obvious to locals, invisible to visitors

There is a simple reason guidebooks leave this out: to the people who live here it is too obvious to be worth explaining.

画
The backstory
An art-supply house in the middle of the book quarter

An art-supply house in the middle of the book quarter. Its stone facade survives intact and draws visitors on its own; locals treat it as the place to go when the bookshops wear you out.

雑
How to order
It is not only for professionals

It is not only for professionals — the ground floor is full of stationery and gifts, with serious art materials on the upper floors and a gallery cafe above that.

What it is

Chiyoda is a ward where government offices meet the old-book quarter. An hour vanishes between entering and leaving. Best not to schedule anything straight afterwards. Stock turns over weekly. One visit is not enough to judge the place. Weekday mornings are calmest; expect to queue at weekend lunch. Leave large bags in a station locker; there is not much room inside. Rather than anchoring your itinerary, it slots naturally into the gaps between other stops.

What to order

Stationery & goods (1F)
Plenty to choose from even if you do not paint. A safe souvenir of Jimbocho.

Professional art supplies (upper floors)
Oils, watercolours, printmaking supplies — the core of a shop running since 1887.

Gallery cafe
On an upper floor. Locals use it as a break between second-hand bookshops.
¥600~

Why locals keep coming

It is curation no chain could imitate — what gets left out matters more than what gets stocked. The depth of stock from years in one niche is different in kind — items you could not know existed via search.
