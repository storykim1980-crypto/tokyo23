Local Knowledge

Obvious to locals, invisible to visitors

There is a simple reason guidebooks leave this out: to the people who live here it is too obvious to be worth explaining.

解
Unwritten rule
Half a chicken arrives deep-fried whole

Half a chicken arrives deep-fried whole, and if you have no idea how to tackle it a staff member will break it down in front of you. But as a rule that is once per group, and not at all when they are busy — and it is over so fast that most people learn nothing. From the second plate you are on your own.

重
How to order
The signature half-chicken is priced by the day, with nothing posted

The signature half-chicken is priced by the day, with nothing posted. The server reels off three or four weights and prices in a single breath, and the trick is to catch it first time — if you cannot, just say &#x27;the last one&#x27;. It must also be ordered one per person; sharing is not allowed.

従
Unwritten rule
A notice at the door refuses anyone who has been drinking

A notice at the door refuses anyone who has been drinking. Many independent shops in Tateishi do the same, which is why a crawl here always starts in a fixed place. Staff direct you precisely — where to sit, where to put your bag, where your shoes go — and following at once makes it easier for everyone. The brusqueness is not hostility.

酢
How to order
Finish the ponzu dish you ordered first and a sauce of spring onion and chilli is left

Finish the ponzu dish you ordered first and a sauce of spring onion and chilli is left in the dish. Regulars keep it back and pour it over the shredded cabbage that comes with the fried chicken, and use it to vary the flavour of the chicken too. And apart from the thickest bones, most of the bird is edible.

What it is

Katsushika is a ward where Shibamata and old-fashioned warmth remain. The kitchen is bigger than the dining area. Sound and smell carry straight over, so the wait never drags. The set with rice and soup is the safe pick — more satisfying than ordering à la carte. Around two in the afternoon, once the lunch peak has passed, is the quietest window. Menus are often Japanese-only; showing a photo or pointing works perfectly well. If you return, come at a different hour — the impression changes considerably.

Why locals keep coming

The layout suits solo diners: mostly counter seats, and nobody encourages you to linger. Prices have barely moved in a decade, meaning the shop has absorbed much of the rise in ingredient costs.
